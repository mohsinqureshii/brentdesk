# Big 5 Construct Saudi 2026 — Editor's reporting notes

Source: Mo Qureshi, on the exhibition floor at Riyadh Front, 30 August –
2 September 2026. Supplied as editorial direction and first-hand context.

## How these may be used

These are the editor's own observations from attending the show. They may
be written as aggregated, first-person editorial observation:

> "Walking the halls of Big 5 Construct Saudi…"
> "One theme was difficult to miss on the exhibition floor…"
> "Across conversations with exhibitors…"
> "A recurring question from international companies was…"
> "What stood out during the show…"

They may NOT be used to:
- invent a named quotation, or attribute a statement to a named person or
  company, unless it is substantiated by material supplied here or a public

- claim BrentDesk met a specific company unless the notes support it — write
  "companies we discovered / followed / that stood out" instead;
- stand alone. Every material claim needs independent verification.

Where there is no named interview, keep the observation aggregated and
anonymous. Never manufacture a survey.

## Recurring themes from the floor

**Localization is the show's strongest theme.** Companies increasingly want
to manufacture locally, establish Saudi operations, use Saudi distributors
or partners, and reduce dependence on imports.

**International manufacturers have moved past "how do we sell here".** They
are asking who to partner with, how procurement actually works, what
localization is expected of them, who the real decision makers are, and how
to build a durable Saudi presence.

**Contractors want measurable operational value, not AI claims.** What they
ask about: fewer breakdowns, faster completion, less rework, lower manpower
dependency, better quality, safer sites, energy savings, lower lifecycle
cost.

**Visible shift from traditional equipment toward automation**, sensors,
robotics, connected equipment, digital monitoring and physical AI.

**The follow-on opportunity is operations, not just construction.** The
larger prize is operating, maintaining and optimising Saudi Arabia's new
assets for decades after handover.

**FM is moving beyond manpower and cleaning** into asset management,
preventive and predictive maintenance, BMS, sensors, work-order systems,
energy management, fire and life safety, condition monitoring and
operational intelligence.

**HVAC is becoming critical infrastructure.** Saudi Arabia is building
real estate, industrial facilities and AI/data-centre capacity at the same
time, and all three create enormous cooling demand.

**The LEAP × Big 5 overlap matters.** One event discusses AI, data centres
and digital infrastructure; the other shows the physical systems that make
a digital economy work — steel, concrete, power, chillers, pumps,
insulation, controls, fire protection, FM and maintenance.

**Saudi manufacturers reject the "cheaper import substitute" framing.**
They emphasise quality, shorter lead times, Saudi technical support, local
production capacity and compliance with Saudi requirements.

**Buyers are asking harder questions**: certification, tested performance,
lifecycle cost, warranties, local after-sales support, spare parts, real
project references.

**The physical show still earns its place.** Buyers can touch equipment,
compare competing products side by side, inspect build quality, and have
technical conversations that are hard to replicate online.

**Modular construction does not remove complexity.** It shifts it earlier —
into design coordination, manufacturing, logistics, sequencing and site
readiness before installation.

**Construction robotics is becoming real rather than theoretical.** The
compelling applications are repetitive, difficult or labour-intensive:
spraying, finishing, grinding, trenching, reinforcement processing,
inspection and monitoring.

**Connected concrete stood out.** Even very traditional materials are
becoming measurable digital assets.

**There is a workforce question underneath the pipeline.** Technology helps,
but training, technical skills and qualified people remain fundamental.

**Procurement and supply-chain resilience are now strategic.** The volume
and speed of Saudi development demand reliable suppliers, local inventory,
logistics, visibility and alternative sourcing.
