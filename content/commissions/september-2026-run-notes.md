## LEAP 2026 — verified figures

- Closed on investments, launches and agreements approaching **$15bn** over
  four days. The 2027 edition returns to **April**.
- **Al Moammar Information Systems (MIS)** — $1.2bn to expand its data
  centre estate to **192MW** of total operational capacity. Founded 1979,
  Riyadh. Signed an MoU in July 2026 for three data centres totalling 48MW.
- **NHC Innovation** — SAR 3.3bn (~$880m) for data centres at **Khuzam
  Digital Valley**, ~200,000 m², targeting **65MW by 2033**. Anchor clients
  NAVER Innovation and BytePlus. The valley was launched at the Real Estate
  Future Forum.
- **Alfanar** — $150m to manufacture data centre components in the Kingdom.
  Separately, its development arm has a $1.4bn programme for four Saudi
  data centres.
- **HPE** — next-generation Saudi Made servers (ProLiant Compute DL360 and
  DL380 Gen12) built at Alfanar's Riyadh facility, **700 servers a month**,
  designed to scale. Programme "Saudi Made, Developed, Deployed" run with
  Intel and the Ministry of Communications and Information Technology, plus
  a Saudi Innovation Centre at HPE's new Riyadh offices with Intel.
- **Saudi Energy** — three agreements supporting digital infrastructure for
  data and AI centres. The first, National Grid SA with Humain, covers
  electricity supply to the Riyadh AI data centres project.
- **stc**, through **center3** — AI-ready capacity starting at 250MW.
  **Zain KSA** signed an MoU with **DataVolt** to co-develop facilities.
- **AWS** — first Saudi cloud region on track for December 2026, backed by
  more than $5.3bn.
- Closing day added $857m; venture capital accounted for ~$293m across 11
  deals (nine rounds totalling $126.15m, plus a fund launch and an existing
  fund expansion worth $166.7m combined).

Sources disagree on whether the MIS and NHC announcements fell on day two
or day three. The Saudi Press Agency release places both on day two and is
the primary source; **do not assert a specific day in copy.**

## Middle East Energy 2026 — verified figures

- 50th edition, more than **1,900 companies** from over **150 countries**,
  around **35,000** professionals. Moved to September for this edition only;
  2027 returns to May (11–13 May).
- Co-located: The Battery Show Middle East, Intersolar Middle East, Energy
  Storage Middle East.
- Energy Club buyers held more than **$98bn** of live projects across the
  Middle East, Africa and South Asia — the largest in the show's 50 years.
- **DEWA** — AED 7bn smart grid programme through **2035**.
- **GCCIA–Oman interconnection** — two 400kV circuits, ~**530km**, Al Sila
  (UAE) to Ibri (Oman); 400kV substations at Ibri and Al Baynounah; a
  compensator station; up to **1,600MW**; ~**$700m**; work began 1 February
  2026; completion expected late 2027. Financing includes the Qatar Fund for
  Development and Sohar International Bank. One source renders the value as
  "$700bn" — that is a typographical error, corroborated elsewhere as $700m.

## Rules for writers on this run

1. **A scheduled article is a preview, never a report.** Gastech and GAIN
   open on 14 and 15 September. Nothing said, signed or announced there
   exists yet. Write from published programmes and confirmed participants,
   flag the file `status: "SCHEDULED"` with a `scheduledReason`, and leave
   outcome coverage to a later batch.
2. **The scheduling horizon is ten days** from the day the gate runs. The
   ingest still holds a scheduled row until its `eventDate` arrives.
3. **Attribute the 35,000 visitor expectation as an expectation** where a
   post-show figure has not been published, as with Big 5.
4. Every English article takes an independently written Arabic counterpart
   in `content/translations/ar/<slug>.json`, which must carry the same
   links, the same HTML tag count and every figure in the English.

## Open items

- **Saudi Industrial Expo venue.** The organiser's site carries both
  "30 Aug – 1 Sept 2026, Riyadh Front Exhibition & Conference Center" on its
  homepage and "RICEC, Riyadh" on its plan-your-visit page, and third-party
  listings additionally circulate 8–10 September dates. The dates are taken
  as **30 August – 1 September**, which is consistent with the archive
  already placing the co-located Saudi Wood Expo in Big 5 week (b5-078).
  The **venue is unresolved** — name the dates, and omit or attribute the
  venue until a primary source settles it.

- **Galaxy Engineering or Galaxy Technology.** The Big 5 organiser's own
  Impact Trail post names **Galaxy Engineering** as the company showing
  Albion Valves' cast-iron range, and galaxy-engineering.com is a Riyadh
  trading and service company carrying valves, pumps and heat exchangers.
  Other coverage and roughly twenty existing archive articles say **Galaxy
  Technology**, and a "Galaxy Technology Co." exists separately. These may
  be two companies or one rendered two ways, and the evidence does not
  settle it. b5-153 uses Galaxy Engineering on the organiser's own wording;
  the existing articles have been left as they are. **A corpus-wide rename
  needs the publisher's decision, not a guess** — renaming twenty articles
  and their Arabic versions on contested evidence would be a worse error
  than the inconsistency it removes.
