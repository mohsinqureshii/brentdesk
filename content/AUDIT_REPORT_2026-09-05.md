# BrentDesk archive audit, 4–5 September 2026

Scope: every English and Arabic article in the archive, its headline, body, SEO fields, structured data and publishing setup, plus the author biographies and house advertisements that sit beside them. Baseline: commit d805eb1 (288 English, 288 Arabic). Result: 288 English, 288 Arabic, 0 removals, 14 active redirects.

## Summary

- Every one of the 288 English and 288 Arabic articles was reviewed individually against the brief, and all 288 are live in both languages. The first pass folded 11 English articles and their Arabic versions into articles carrying the same story; at the publisher's instruction all 11 were then restored, each rebuilt as a distinct piece with an argument the article it had been merged into does not make, and each with independently written Arabic.
- Every headline and SEO title is in sentence case. Listicle framing is gone from headlines, slugs and bodies — including in the restored articles, four of which came back under clearer slugs with 301s from their old URLs. The banned puff words are absent except for five uses the facts require: the industrial noun "groundbreakings", the geological term "volcanogenic massive sulphide", and one attributed direct quote.
- Twenty-one Arabic bodies were written afresh as independent Arabic journalism rather than translated, and 18 Arabic headlines were rewritten. One company was named wrongly in Arabic (Arabian Pipes Company) and two brand renderings were inconsistent across the corpus; all three are corrected.
- The six items the first pass flagged for a decision are closed: the utility name was verified against the Saudi Press Agency and kept with the source added, the disputed supplier figure was resolved and aligned, British spelling was adopted as house style and applied, the author biographies were rewritten and now serve in both languages, the house advertisements were translated, and the attendance framing was kept as commissioned coverage. Images remain open at the publisher's request.
- The publishing layer was fixed where the rendered pages showed defects: Arabic pages carried English URLs in their share and structured data, Arabic redirects returned 404, page views re-stamped the modified date, re-ingesting an edit re-dated its publication, a retired article could not be republished by shipping its file again, articles without an image shipped no share image, author URLs pointed at numeric ids, and every public page's browser title gained a second "| BrentDesk" the moment React hydrated over a correct server-rendered one.

## Publishing setup changes

- **Merged rows are unpublished, not just redirected.** Deleting an article file used to leave its row published, listed and indexed at the old URL. The ingest moves a row whose URL is a redirect source and whose slug left the archive to the archived status, marks it noindex and clears its publication date. Verified in the first pass: 11 rows archived on the first boot, absent from the article sitemap.
- **A restored article is republished, not silently left dark.** Restoring those 11 exposed the other half of that mechanism: a row with no publication date took the ordinary edit path, which deliberately preserves publishedAt, so shipping the file again updated the text and left the article unpublished. The ingest now treats a row whose publishedAt is NULL as a republication and gives it today's date. Verified: 288 published, 0 rows with a null publication date, 0 noindex.
- **A redirect can be retired without being forgotten.** Seven of the restored articles came back at the URLs their redirects pointed away from. Rather than deleting those rules, content/redirects.json marks them `"active": false`: the ingest sets isActive to 0 and the retire pass skips them, so the record of the merge survives and turning a rule back on is one flag. Verified: 28 rules on file, 14 active, and all 22 restored URLs answer 200 in both languages.
- **Renamed slugs keep their row.** A rename is declared with previousSlugs; the ingest updates the existing row in place so its id, translations, related-article links and history survive. Verified: 0 rows created across both passes, renamed rows carry their new slugs, old paths 301.
- **Boot ingest follows a content fingerprint.** The boot check compared row counts and could not see a release that only edited or merged. It compares a fingerprint of the bundled archive with the one last published. Verified: the restoration boot ingested, the next boot reported nothing to do, and updatedAt did not move.
- **Publication and modified dates stay honest.** Re-ingesting a corrected article does not rewrite publishedAt, and a page view does not bump updatedAt, so dateModified in the NewsArticle data changes only when the article does.
- **Arabic pages describe themselves as Arabic.** og:url, twitter:url, the NewsArticle url and mainEntityOfPage, the breadcrumb items and inLanguage are rewritten for the language served, alongside the existing canonical, hreflang and og:locale handling. The section name in the breadcrumb and articleSection is the Arabic category name.
- **Arabic redirects answer.** The redirects lookup ran after the router's language pass-through, so every /ar/ redirect was a 404. The lookup runs first; a test covers the prefixed case.
- **Author biographies and house advertisements are translatable.** Both live outside the article tables, so both rendered in English on Arabic pages. Users and ad creatives are now furniture the translation overlay covers, keyed by username and creative name; the author router, the article and author SSR paths and the ad slot resolver all pass the request locale through. Verified: /author/mo-qureshi and its Arabic URL serve different biographies and job titles, and the sidebar and in-content house ads serve Arabic copy.
- **Browser titles stopped doubling the masthead.** Ten public pages passed a title that already ended in "| BrentDesk" to a component that appended it again, and the site settings context then overwrote the homepage title with the bare publication name. The component appends the masthead only when the page has not named it, the settings context renames rather than replaces, and the title-case tagline used in page titles now lives beside the sentence-case one in shared/publication.ts so the server-rendered title and the hydrated title cannot drift. Verified in Chromium across ten routes: every hydrated title matches the served one.
- **Share images and author URLs.** Articles without a featured image carry the site image in og:image, twitter:image and the NewsArticle. Author URLs use the byline's username, which the ingest fills in when the seed never set it.
- **Editorial gates.** The article gate takes today's date from the clock instead of a constant that expired the day after it was written. Its list of deliberately retired commissions is empty again now that 035 is back. Both gates pass with zero errors and zero warnings.

## Decisions taken on the flagged items

- **The utility name "Saudi Energy" — verified and kept.** Saudi Electricity Company rebranded to Saudi Energy on 26 February 2026, confirmed against the Saudi Press Agency. The nine articles that use the name keep it, and the SPA announcement was added to their sources.
- **The disputed supplier figure — resolved.** Brickeye's own claim is more than 1,000 projects across more than 20 countries; the seven English articles and their Arabic versions that carried a larger number were aligned to it. CMCI's separate claim of more than 3,000 regional projects is a different company's figure and stands as published.
- **Spelling — British, and applied.** The corpus mixed -ise and -ize. British spelling is the house rule, normalised across 14 English files; URLs and quoted company names were left untouched.
- **Author biographies — rewritten, in both languages.** Five biographies (an editor, an industry correspondent, the research desk, the joint byline and the staff byline) replace the seeded placeholder text, and each has an Arabic version written for an Arabic reader rather than translated. The ingest refreshes a biography that is still the seeded one, so no operator edit is overwritten.
- **House advertisements — translated.** The three house creatives carry Arabic headline, description and call to action.
- **Attendance framing — kept.** The 52 Big 5 Construct Saudi pieces stay as commissioned coverage under their existing bylines; no new attendance claim was added anywhere in this audit.
- **Images — deferred at the publisher's request.** No article carries a featured image, so alt text and image structured data still cannot be audited. Share cards fall back to the site image.

## Not recoverable

- **Publication dates before this release.** Until the first pass, every deploy that changed an article also re-stamped its publishedAt, so datePublished on the live archive reflected deploy dates rather than first publication, and the original timestamps were never retained. The defect is fixed going forward; the 11 restored articles are dated to the day they were restored, which is truthful. eventDate, which readers see, was never affected.
